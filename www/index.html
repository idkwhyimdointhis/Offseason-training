Coach's Note: At 16–17 with ~3 months of consistent gym training, you are in a critical development window. Your body will respond rapidly to structured overload. The priority is building a genuine physical foundation — strength, speed, and football conditioning — while keeping your technical work sharp. Every session has a purpose.
Biggest Offseason Mistake to Avoid: Only doing long slow runs. A CM/DM covers 10–13km per game but at varying intensities — short sprints, direction changes, recovery runs. Your conditioning must mirror that. We build your engine correctly.

STRENGTH SYSTEM
Progressive overload structure — every 2 weeks the stimulus shifts
Principle: At 16–17 with your training age, you will get strong quickly. Focus on compound movements, consistent progressive overload, and keeping injury prevention (Nordic curls, single-leg work) non-negotiable every week.

PROGRESSIVE OVERLOAD METHOD

  • Add 2.5–5kg to compound lifts when you hit the top end of the rep range across all sets
  • Log every session — weight, reps, and how it felt (RPE 1–10). Non-negotiable for progression
  • If a lift drops in quality (form breaks), do not add weight — add a rep instead
  • Deload in Week 7: drop all weights to 60% of your Phase 3 loads, same reps — flush fatigue
  • Prioritise sleep. At 16–17 you release growth hormone during deep sleep — this is when you actually get stronger
SPEED & POWER SYSTEM
Acceleration, top speed, deceleration, reactive agility, jump ability
Key Principle: Speed is a skill. You cannot train it when tired. Sprint sessions always come after a proper warmup, never at the end of a fatiguing session. Full recovery between reps = faster, more effective adaptation.

REPEAT SPRINT ABILITY (RSA) — Thursday Focus

  • Phase 1: 6×40m at 85% — 2 min rest. Goal: aerobic speed endurance base
  • Phase 2: 8×30m at 90% — 90s rest. Goal: reduced recovery tolerance (harder)
  • Phase 3: 10×20m at 95%+ — 60s rest. Goal: true RSA — game-realistic demands
  • Track your split times each session. RSA is measured by how little speed you lose from rep 1 to rep 10

DECELERATION & CHANGE OF DIRECTION

  • 5-10-5 shuttle: 3 cones, 5 yards each side. Time yourself weekly — this measures COD speed
  • T-Test: forward 10m, side shuffle 5m left, side shuffle 10m right, side shuffle 5m left, back 10m
  • Reactive drills: partner points left/right — you decelerate and sprint. Game-realistic because decisions happen under pressure
  • Teaching deceleration: land on full foot (not toe), knee slightly bent, hips back — avoid ACL stress position

TECHNICAL DEVELOPMENT
CM/DM specific — ball mastery, passing, positioning, weak foot, tactical IQ
Philosophy: Technical sessions in the offseason should be deliberate practice — not just kicking a ball around. Every session has a specific skill target. You are programming your nervous system through repetition. Weak foot gets equal time to strong foot.

DAILY MINIMUMS

  • 10 min ball mastery daily — inside/outside rolls, toe taps, Maradona, scissors, cruyff turns. Do this before any session
  • Weak foot — 5 min minimum every day — passing against a wall, receiving and turning, finishing. This compounds over 7 weeks
  • Film study — 3× per week, 20–30 min — watch elite CMs (Rodri, Pedri, Bellingham). Study positioning, movement off the ball, pressing triggers

FILM STUDY TARGETS — DECISION MAKING

  • Watch how elite CMs scan before receiving the ball — count shoulder checks per possession
  • Study pressing triggers: when do they press? Ball played sideways to a CB? That's a trigger. Train yourself to recognize them
  • Positional shadows: how does Rodri position his body to block passing lanes while being available to receive?
  • Transition moments: where does a CM move the instant possession is won or lost?
  • Write 2–3 observations after each film session — makes it active learning, not passive watching
RECOVERY SYSTEM
Recovery is training. You don't improve during sessions — you improve during recovery from them.
At 16–17 your recovery rate is exceptional — but only if you support it. Sleep deprivation at your age directly limits growth hormone release, reduces strength gains by up to 30%, and slows sprint adaptation. Sleep is the #1 recovery tool.

SLEEP PROTOCOL

  • Target: 9 hours per night — you are still growing. 8 hours is the minimum. 9–10 is optimal
  • Same wake time every day — even weekends. Regulates cortisol rhythm which affects recovery
  • No screens 45 min before bed — blue light suppresses melatonin. Use this time for film study on a dim screen or stretching
  • Cool, dark room. 17–19°C is optimal for deep sleep quality
  • Track subjective sleep quality (1–10) each morning in your log alongside training data

WEDNESDAY RECOVERY SESSION — 30 MIN

  • 5 min: Light walk or easy bike to increase blood flow without loading muscles
  • 10 min: Full body mobility — hip circles, thoracic rotations, ankle CARs, shoulder circles
  • 10 min: Static stretching — hamstrings 45s, hip flexors 45s, quads 45s, calves 45s, chest 30s
  • 5 min: Foam rolling — quads, IT band, calves, thoracic spine
  • Optional: Cold water immersion (10 min, 10–15°C) or contrast shower to reduce inflammation

WEEKLY INJURY PREVENTION NON-NEGOTIABLES

  • Nordic Hamstring Curls every Tuesday — the single most evidence-based exercise for hamstring injury prevention in footballers (reduces risk ~51%)
  • Hip stability work weekly — clamshells, single-leg glute bridges, lateral band walks before any lower session
  • Ankle proprioception — single-leg balance (30s each, eyes closed) every warmup
  • Eccentric loading — slow 4-count lowering phase on squats, RDLs, and Nordic curls protects tendons
  • Never skip warmup — cold muscle sprinting is how hamstrings tear. Non-negotiable

DELOAD — WEEK 7

  • Reduce all gym volume by 40% — same movements, same intensity, fewer sets
  • Reduce conditioning volume by 40% — keep sprints sharp but do fewer reps
  • Increase technical work — arrive at preseason with sharp touch and fresh legs
  • Prioritise sleep and nutrition this week more than any other
  • Do not try to cram extra sessions — that defeats the deload purpose
PERFORMANCE NUTRITION
Fuel your training. Recover faster. Build a stronger body. Simple, practical principles.
Context: At 16–17 in hard training, you need to eat enough — most young athletes under-eat and then wonder why they're not getting stronger. Food is the raw material for everything: muscle, speed, recovery, focus.
Protein
1.8–2.2g
Per kg bodyweight daily. Chicken, eggs, beef, fish, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese. Spread across 4–5 meals — not one big serving.
Carbohydrates
5–7g
Per kg on training days. Rice, oats, pasta, potatoes, fruit. This is your fuel — don't fear carbs. Reduce slightly on rest days.
Healthy Fats
1–1.2g
Per kg daily. Avocado, olive oil, nuts, oily fish. Support hormone production — critical for testosterone at your age.
Hydration
3–4L
Water per day minimum. Add electrolytes (sodium, potassium) on heavy training days — especially after sprint sessions. Urine should be pale yellow.
Before Bed
30–40g
Casein protein before sleep (cottage cheese, Greek yogurt, casein shake). Feeds muscle protein synthesis during the 9 hours of sleep you're getting.
Pre-Training
60–90
Minutes before training: 40–60g carbs + 20g protein. Banana + protein shake is fine. Don't train fasted for high-intensity sessions.

POST-SESSION RECOVERY WINDOW

  • Within 30–45 minutes after session: 30–40g protein + 60–80g carbs. This window matters most on heavy training days
  • Chocolate milk is actually a legitimate post-training option — 3:1 carb to protein ratio, cheap, effective
  • Creatine monohydrate (3–5g daily) is safe, evidence-based, and highly effective for strength and sprint performance at your age. Speak to a doctor or nutritionist if unsure
  • Avoid ultra-processed food on training days — quality fuel = quality output

Test at start of Week 1, end of Week 4, and end of Week 7. Track everything.
Why measure? Without data, you don't know if the plan is working. These tests take 30–45 minutes and tell you exactly where you've improved. Run the same tests each time under the same conditions (same time of day, same warm-up).

TRAINING LOG — WHAT TO TRACK DAILY

  • Gym: Every exercise, weight used, reps completed, RPE (1–10)
  • Speed work: Distance, reps, and subjective effort — did you feel fast?
  • Recovery: Sleep hours (1–10 quality), soreness level (1–10), energy (1–10)
  • Technical: What you worked on and one thing you want to improve next session
  • Nutrition: Simple check — did you hit protein target? Did you hydrate enough?
  • Review weekly — if energy is consistently 4/10 or lower, you may be under-recovering. Adjust load or sleep
